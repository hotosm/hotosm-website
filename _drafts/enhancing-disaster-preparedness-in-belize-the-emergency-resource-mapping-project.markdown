Belize, like many other Caribbean countries, faces significant risks from natural disasters such as hurricanes, floods, and forest fires. These events can have devastating impacts on communities, infrastructure, and the economy. Recognizing the critical need for improved disaster preparedness, the Emergency Resource Mapping Project was launched in Belize. This initiative aimed to enhance the country's disaster response capabilities by providing accurate and up-to-date information on available emergency resources. The project was a collaboration between the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT), the [Caribbean Disaster Emergency Management Agency (CDEMA)](https://www.cdema.org/), and Belize's [National Emergency Management Organization](https://site.nemo.org.bz/) (NEMO).

# Project Overview

The Emergency Resource Mapping Project focused on mapping key emergency resources in Belize to strengthen disaster response and preparedness. The project was implemented in four key areas: Belize City, Belmopan City, San Ignacio Town, and Santa Elena Town. These areas were selected due to their vulnerability to natural disasters and their importance in the national disaster response network.

The project's objectives were: first, it aimed to develop detailed maps of emergency resources in the selected areas. These maps would include critical infrastructure such as communication towers, fire stations, roads, airstrips, ports, drainage systems, bridges, and hazardous locations. By identifying and cataloging these resources and being able to view this data geographically using open-source software, the project sought to provide decision-makers and stakeholders with the necessary information to respond effectively in the event of a disaster.

Second, the project aimed to increase the availability of geospatial data for disaster management. This data would be accessible not only to government agencies and emergency management organizations like NEMO but also to the public. By making this information publicly available in platforms such as OpenStreetMap, the project hoped to raise awareness of disaster risks.

Third, the project sought to build local capacity by training stakeholders in the use of open-source GIS tools for emergency mapping. By empowering local communities with the skills and tools needed to conduct their own mapping activities, the project aimed to foster a culture of open mapping in Belize. This would ensure that the country remains prepared for future disasters by continuously updating and improving its disaster preparedness data.

Finally, the project aimed to raise public awareness of disaster risks and foster a culture of open mapping in Belize. By involving local volunteers in the mapping process and making the data publicly available, the project sought to encourage a more proactive approach to disaster preparedness.

# Project Implementation

The implementation of the Emergency Resource Mapping Project involved a combination of remote and field mapping activities. Using OpenStreetMap (OSM) and other open-source tools, the project team was able to gather, validate, and analyze data on critical infrastructure and hazard-prone areas.

Remote mapping was conducted using OSM plugins such as Quick OSM. This allowed the team to gather existing datasets on roads and waterways. These datasets were then validated during field mapping activities, where volunteers collected additional data using the Kobo Toolbox, an open-source tool for data collection in challenging environments. The data collected included information on points of interest such as communication towers, fire stations, airstrips, ports, and bridges. The data was then compiled and shared in various formats, including shapefiles and GeoJSON files, with the project's stakeholders.

The Kobo Toolbox was instrumental in the data collection process. Digital survey forms were created to standardize the data collection process, ensuring accuracy and consistency across the different areas being mapped. These forms included fields for the name, location, and attributes of each point of interest, as well as geolocation coordinates and photos. The data collectors were trained in the use of the Kobo Toolbox, and their data submissions were monitored in real-time through the web interface.

Once the data was collected, it was analyzed and managed using QGIS, an open-source GIS software. QGIS allowed the project team to visualize the data collected, perform spatial analysis, and prepare the geospatial data for further use. For example, the project team used QGIS to extract data on drainage systems in Belize City and overlay it with other geographic information to create comprehensive maps of flood-prone areas.

# Key Findings and Challenges

The Emergency Resource Mapping Project successfully mapped key emergency resources in Belize City, Belmopan City, San Ignacio Town, and Santa Elena Town. The data collected revealed significant gaps in resource distribution, particularly in areas prone to flooding. By identifying these gaps, the project highlighted the need for improved infrastructure and resource allocation in these areas.

One of the major challenges encountered during the project was the difficulty in obtaining certain types of data, such as the height of communication towers. This information was often held by private companies, which were reluctant to share it without formal requests from government agencies. Despite these challenges, the project was able to collect a substantial amount of data, which was then shared with stakeholders and made publicly available.

# Capacity Building and Public Awareness

A key component of the Emergency Resource Mapping Project was the training and capacity-building activities conducted with local stakeholders. A training session was held at NEMO headquarters in Belmopan City, where participants were trained in the use of the Kobo Toolbox and introduced to OpenStreetMap (OSM) and other open-source tools. The training was attended by university students and virtual volunteers, who then participated in the mapping activities.
The involvement of local volunteers was crucial to the success of the project. By engaging university students and community members in the mapping process, the project was able to raise public awareness of disaster risks and foster a culture of open mapping in Belize. This heightened awareness will help communities better understand their local emergency infrastructure and encourage proactive measures for disaster mitigation and response.

# Recommendations and Future Directions

The Emergency Resource Mapping Project has made significant strides in improving disaster preparedness in Belize. However, the project also identified areas where further work is needed. One of the key recommendations is the continual updating of the mapped data. As new infrastructure is built and existing resources change, it is essential that the maps are kept up-to-date to ensure their effectiveness in disaster response.

Another recommendation is to expand the mapping coverage to other towns and regions in Belize. While the project focused on four key areas, there are many other vulnerable areas in the country that would benefit from detailed emergency resource mapping. Expanding the coverage of the project would help to ensure that all communities in Belize are prepared for future disasters.

Finally, the project recommends ongoing capacity-building activities to ensure that local stakeholders have the skills and tools needed to conduct their own mapping activities. By continuing to train and engage local communities in the mapping process, Belize can build a more resilient society that is better prepared to respond to natural disasters.

# Conclusion

The Emergency Resource Mapping Project in Belize represents a significant step forward in enhancing the country's disaster preparedness and response capabilities. By providing accurate and up-to-date information on emergency resources, building local capacity, and raising public awareness, the project has laid the foundation for a more resilient Belize. As the country continues to face the challenges posed by natural disasters, initiatives like this will be crucial in ensuring the safety and well-being of its communities.
